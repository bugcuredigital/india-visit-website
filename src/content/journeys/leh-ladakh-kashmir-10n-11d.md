Ladakh is the least Indian-feeling part of India: a high cold desert in the
rain shadow of the Himalaya, Tibetan Buddhist in its monasteries and its
calendar, and reached by a flight that drops you at eleven and a half thousand
feet with no warning. This journey does it properly. It gives the first day to
acclimatising, the next three to the monasteries of the Indus valley — Shey,
Thiksey, Hemis, Alchi, Lamayuru — and then does what most Ladakh itineraries
do not: it drives out, west over the Zoji La, into Kashmir.

That second half is the reason for the route. Srinagar's lakes and Mughal
gardens and the meadow at Gulmarg are the green answer to Ladakh's rock, and
seeing both in one trip is the kind of contrast that stays with people. The
road is open from June to September, which is when we run it; every flight,
transfer and permit is handled, and the pace is set by the altitude rather than
the schedule.
