Most Golden Triangle journeys begin in Delhi. This one begins four hundred
kilometres further north-west, at the Golden Temple in Amritsar, and takes the
long way round: Le Corbusier's planned capital at Chandigarh, the Ganges where
it leaves the hills at Rishikesh and Haridwar, and only then the capital, the
pink city and the Taj. It is a route through the Sikh, Hindu and Mughal north
in the order the country lays them out.

Twelve days is what that needs. The drives are broken by stepwells, border
ceremonies and river ghats rather than endured, and the three cities of the
triangle get the two nights each that let you see them at the right hour — the
Taj in the morning, Amer before the heat, the Jama Masjid when the courtyard is
quiet. Every transfer, guide and reservation is handled.
