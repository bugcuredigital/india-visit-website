      are a day's drive away in either direction, and they deserve you awake.

  - dayNo: 3
    title: Ajanta Caves — A Day Inside Living Art
    narrative: >-
      Today is given entirely to Ajanta. Carved between the 2nd century BC and
      the 6th century AD, these monolithic caverns hold some of the finest
      surviving classical Indian painting and sculpture anywhere — Buddhist
      stupas, pillared prayer halls, and walls and ceilings alive with two
      millennia of devotional art. The site was lost to dense jungle for
      centuries before its rediscovery, which is precisely why so much of the
      paintwork survives.
    overnight: Overnight in Aurangabad
  - dayNo: 4
    title: Ellora Caves — Three Faiths, One Hillside
    transport: { mode: overnight-train, detail: Overnight train — to Hyderabad }
    narrative: >-
      Today belongs to Ellora, cut into the Charanandri Hills by the Kalachuri,
      Chalukya and Rashtrakuta dynasties and known locally as Verul Leni. Its 34
      caves set Hindu, Buddhist and Jain monuments side by side on a single
      UNESCO-listed hillside — a piece of religious coexistence in stone that has
      no real equivalent. Do not miss the Vishvakarma Cave, nicknamed the
      Carpenter's Cave, or the Dashavatara Cave dedicated to Vishnu. In the
      evening, board the overnight train south.
    overnight: Overnight aboard the train

    narrative: >-
      Arrive in the Telangana capital, long known as the city of Mughals and

      Charminar, raised by Qutb Shah when he moved his capital here, its minarets
      looking out across the old town. Then Chowmahalla Palace, seat of the
      Nizams, where Persian-Arabic architecture unfolds across four palaces,

      from the water — worth taking a boat out for. For something entirely

India rarely reveals herself all at once — she unfolds, cave by cave, temple by
temple, palace by palace. This thirteen-day journey was designed for travellers
who want more than a checklist: a passage through two-thousand-year-old rock-cut
sanctuaries, Mughal and Nizami courts, Dravidian temple cities carved from single
boulders, and the palaces of maharajas who once ruled kingdoms the size of small
nations.

You will trace the chisel-marks of monks who cut Buddhist prayer halls into solid
basalt at Ajanta and Ellora, stand beneath minarets raised when Hyderabad was
young, watch the tide roll over shore temples older than most nations, and walk
the marble halls of Mysore's royal family. This is a fully escorted itinerary —
private transfers, English-speaking local guides, and a pace built for genuine
discovery rather than a rushed checklist. Every stop is handled for you, so all
that is left to do is arrive, look up, and be amazed.
