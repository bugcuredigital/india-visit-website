Most first trips to India go west and south. This one goes east, into the seven
states that sit beyond the narrow corridor of land connecting them to the rest of
the country — and into landscapes that look nothing like the India of the
brochures.

Meghalaya means "abode of clouds", and it earns the name: gorges and plunge
waterfalls, limestone caves, and a river at Dawki so clear that the boats on it
appear to be suspended in air. Then the road drops back down to the Brahmaputra
plains and Kaziranga, where two-thirds of the world's one-horned rhinoceros live
in grass tall enough to hide an elephant. Seven days, one long driving day, and
a part of the country that very few visitors reach.
