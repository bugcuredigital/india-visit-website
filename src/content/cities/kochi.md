intro:
  - >-
    Kochi is less a city than a set of islands and peninsulas that learned to
    trade with each other. Arabs came for pepper, then the Chinese, then the
    Portuguese in 1500, then the Dutch, then the British — and unusually, none
    of them erased what the last lot built. The result is a small stretch of
    waterfront where a 16th-century Portuguese church, a Dutch palace, a
    synagogue and a working fishing beach sit within a mile of one another.
  - >-
    Fort Kochi is where visitors spend their time, and it rewards being walked
    rather than driven: low buildings, tiled roofs, a lot of shade, and the
    cantilevered Chinese fishing nets on the shoreline that are still lowered
    and raised by hand. Across the water, Ernakulam is the modern city that
    actually does the business.
  - >-
    For most of our travellers Kochi is the beginning of Kerala rather than the
    whole of it — a day or two here before the backwaters, or a last day after
    coming back off a houseboat.

practicalNotes:
  - icon: plane
    label: Getting there
    text: >-
      Cochin International is 30 km north of Fort Kochi, roughly an hour by
      road, with direct flights from the Gulf, Singapore and Colombo as well as
      the Indian metros. It is also the usual arrival point for a Kerala trip,
      which is why most of our southern itineraries begin here.
  - icon: pace
    label: Getting around
    text: >-
      Fort Kochi is walkable end to end and pleasantly so. The public ferry
      between Fort Kochi and Ernakulam takes about twenty minutes, costs almost
      nothing, and is a far better way to cross than the bridge. A car is worth
      having only for the airport and for trips out of town.
  - icon: calendar
    label: Best time
    text: >-
      October to March is dry and warm. The southwest monsoon arrives in June
      and is heavy through July and August — some travellers come for exactly
      that, and for the Ayurveda season that goes with it, but it is not the
      time for walking the fort.

faq:
  - question: How long should I spend in Kochi?
    answer: >-
      One full day covers Fort Kochi, Mattancherry and a Kathakali performance
      comfortably. Two lets you take the ferry across, spend a morning without
      a plan, and treat the city as somewhere you stayed rather than somewhere
      you stopped.
  - question: What is the difference between Kochi and Cochin?
    answer: >-
      They are the same place. Cochin is the anglicised colonial spelling; the
      city was officially renamed Kochi in 1996. You will still see Cochin on
      the airport code, on older signage, and in a good deal of travel writing.
  - question: Should I see Kochi before or after the backwaters?
    answer: >-
      Before, usually. Arriving at Cochin airport and spending a night or two
      in Fort Kochi lets you get over the flight somewhere quiet and walkable,
      and the backwaters then feel like the reward rather than the arrival.
  - question: Is a houseboat booked from Kochi?
    answer: >-
      The houseboats themselves are mostly based in Alappuzha and Kumarakom,
      an hour and a half to two hours south, and that is where you board. We
      arrange the transfer as part of the itinerary, so there is nothing to
      book separately once you are there.
  - question: Can you see a Kathakali performance without sitting through several hours?
    answer: >-
      Yes — the shows put on for visitors run about an hour and a half, with
      the makeup applied on stage beforehand, which is worth arriving for. The
      full traditional form runs all night and is a different proposition
      entirely; if that is what you want, tell us and we will find one.
