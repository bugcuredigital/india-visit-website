intro:
  - >-
    Jaipur is unusual among Indian cities in having been designed. In 1727
    Maharaja Sawai Jai Singh II moved his capital down from the hills at Amer
    and laid out a new city on a grid of nine rectangular sectors, with streets
    wide enough for elephants and a wall around the whole of it. Most of that
    plan is still legible, which is why the old city reads as one composition
    rather than as a collection of monuments.
  - >-
    The pink came later — an 1876 paint job for a royal visit, in the colour of
    welcome — and it stuck. What visitors tend to remember is not any single
    building but the density: a fort on the ridge, an observatory measuring the
    sky in stone, a palace facade built so that women of the court could watch
    the street without being seen from it, and bazaars that have been selling
    the same things in the same lanes for three hundred years.
  - >-
    Two days is enough to see it. Three is enough to enjoy it, and to get out to
    Amer in the early light before the coaches arrive.

  - icon: pace
    label: Getting around
    text: >-
      A car and driver for the day is how the city is normally done, since the
      forts are spread out and the heat is not. The old city itself is best on
      foot: the grid makes it hard to get lost, and the bazaars only work at
      walking pace.
  - icon: calendar
    label: Best time
    text: >-
      October to March, comfortably. April and May are genuinely hot — over
      40°C by afternoon — and July to September brings the monsoon, which is
      dramatic but limits the time you can spend outdoors.

faq:
  - question: How many days do I need in Jaipur?
    answer: >-
      Two full days covers Amer Fort, the City Palace, Jantar Mantar and the
      old city, at a pace that does not feel like a checklist. A third day lets
      you get out to Nahargarh or Jal Mahal, spend an unhurried morning in the
      bazaars, or simply start later.
  - question: Is Jaipur worth visiting on its own, or only as part of the Golden Triangle?
    answer: >-
      It stands on its own, and a number of our travellers fly in and out of
      Jaipur for a Rajasthan-only trip. That said, most people see it as the
      third corner of the Golden Triangle with Delhi and Agra, which is the
      most efficient way to get three very different cities into under a week.
  - question: When is the best time of year to visit Jaipur?
    answer: >-
      October to March. The weather is dry and the days are warm rather than
      hot, which matters when a morning at Amer Fort involves a good deal of
      walking and climbing in the open.
  - question: Can I ride an elephant up to Amer Fort?
    answer: >-
      We do not arrange elephant rides. The practice is contested on welfare
      grounds and we would rather not be part of it; the walk up takes about
      fifteen minutes, and a jeep is available for anyone who would find that
      difficult.
  - question: What is Jaipur best for buying?
    answer: >-
      Gemstones and silver in Johari Bazaar, block-printed and bandhani cloth
      in Bapu Bazaar, and blue pottery, which is a Jaipur speciality. If you
      are buying stones seriously, tell us in advance and we will arrange a
      dealer we have used for years rather than leaving it to chance.
