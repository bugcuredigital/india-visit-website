Ladakh is the one region we sell where the calendar decides almost everything.
It sits at eleven and a half thousand feet in the rain shadow of the Himalaya,
the roads in and out are closed by snow for half the year, and the two lakes
everyone wants to see are at the end of passes that open late and close early.
So the question of when to go is not a matter of preference. Here is how the
year actually works.

## The short version

**June to September** is the season. Within it, **late June and September**
are the months we would pick: the passes are open, the crowds of the Indian
school holidays have not arrived or have gone, and the light is at its best.
Outside the season, Leh itself is reachable by air but the region is not.

## Month by month

**January and February.** Deep winter. Leh is bitterly cold, most hotels are
closed, and every road out is shut. The one reason to come is the Chadar trek
along the frozen Zanskar river, which is a serious undertaking rather than a
holiday.

**March and April.** Still cold, still closed. The Srinagar–Leh road over the
Zoji La usually opens in late April or May, weather depending; the Manali road
later. A few travellers fly in for the quiet. It is not a first-visit month.

**May.** The transition. The passes begin to open, the lakes are still icy at
the edges, and nights are cold. Good for the monasteries; uncertain for
Pangong and Nubra.

**June.** The season opens properly. Days are warm, the passes are reliably
clear, and Pangong and Nubra are reachable. The second half of the month is
one of the two windows we favour.

**July and August.** Peak season — warm, dry (Ladakh sits behind the monsoon,
which is the point of it), and busy. The Hemis festival falls in July. If you
are travelling in these months, book well ahead; the good hotels in Leh fill.

**September.** The other window. The crowds thin, the air clears, the poplars
turn, and the passes are still open. If you have any flexibility at all, aim
here.

**October.** The end of the season. Early October can be beautiful and empty;
by the end of the month the passes are closing and the cold has arrived. Only
with flexible dates.

**November and December.** Closed. Leh by air only, and no reason to.

## What the altitude does to the plan

Whatever month you choose, the first two days are the same. You fly into Leh
and you do very little, because your body has had no time to adjust and it
will let you know. Day three stays at the town's own altitude. Only after that
does the itinerary climb — to the monasteries up the Indus, and then over the
passes.

We will not sell a Ladakh journey without those days built in, and we say so on
every itinerary. The travellers who skip them are the ones who lose the two
that follow.

## Ladakh and Kashmir together

The route we run most often does something most Ladakh itineraries do not: it
drives out. West along the Indus to Alchi and Lamayuru, a night in Kargil, and
then over the Zoji La into Kashmir — Srinagar's lakes and Mughal gardens, and
the meadow at Gulmarg. The rock of Ladakh and the green of the Kashmir valley in
one trip is the contrast people remember, and it is only possible between June
and September, when the pass is open. Which brings the answer back to where it
started.
