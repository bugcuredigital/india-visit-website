The Palace on Wheels is the train most people picture when they picture a
luxury train in India, and the questions we are asked about it are always the
same: what is the route, what is the difference between the cabins, how does
the booking actually work, and what happens if plans change. This guide
answers all four, in the order you will meet them.

## The route

The train leaves Delhi and returns to it, and in the week between it threads
together the places that made Rajasthan's reputation. In order: **Jaipur**,
the pink city; **Sawai Madhopur**, for a morning safari on the edge of
Ranthambore, and **Chittorgarh**, the greatest of the Rajput forts, the same
day; **Udaipur** and a boat on Lake Pichola; **Jaisalmer** and a camel ride on
the dunes; **Jodhpur** beneath Mehrangarh; **Bharatpur** and the bird
sanctuary, then **Agra** and the Taj Mahal; and back to Delhi on the eighth
morning.

The rhythm is the appeal as much as the route. The train moves at night, you
wake somewhere new, and the day's excursion, meals, guide and entrance fees
are all arranged before you board. You unpack once.

## The four cabin categories

Every cabin on the train shares the journey inclusions — accommodation, all
meals on board, the excursions, the entrances, the cultural programmes, the
camel, the boat and the rickshaw. What separates the categories is how you
travel *off* the train, and a few comforts on it.

- **Deluxe Cabin.** The base category. Excursions are by coach with a shared
  guide.
- **Super Deluxe Cabin.** A private car — an Innova or similar — for the
  excursions, a dedicated private guide, and one thirty-minute spa session
  for a couple.
- **Suite.** The private car and private guide, plus Indian brands of liquor
  and beer included, subject to stock.
- **Presidential Suite.** A luxury private car — a Fortuner or similar — the
  private guide, Indian *and* foreign brands of liquor and beer, and the spa
  session.

For most travellers the meaningful step is from Deluxe to Super Deluxe: the
private car and guide change the character of every excursion, and a week of
them is the difference between touring and being taken round.

## How a booking is confirmed

The process is the operator's, and it runs in six steps.

1. **Select and finalise.** We confirm your departure and cabin category and
   share the booking form together with the cancellation terms. Passport
   copies are needed for each traveller's name, nationality and number.
2. **Pay the advance.** Forty per cent against the invoice secures the
   booking. If your international flights are already booked, share the
   details now.
3. **Written confirmation.** Once the advance is received you get a
   confirmation email and letter, with a breakdown of the amount received and
   the balance due.
4. **Adjust the details.** Tell us what you would like added or changed and
   we check what is possible with the operator — some changes cannot be
   accommodated on a fixed-departure train.
5. **Clear the balance** at least 95 days before arrival in India. We send a
   receipt.
6. **Arrival assistance.** About a week before departure we share who will
   meet you at the airport and the name of your guest relations manager.

## What happens if plans change

Cancellation requests must be made in writing. The charge depends on when we
are told: a minimum of **15%** of the ticket value more than 95 days before
departure, **30%** between 65 and 94 days, **55%** between 35 and 64 days,
and **100%** inside 34 days or for a no-show. A journey ended part way through
is treated the same as a no-show for the unused portion.

Amendments need the operator's approval and are generally considered only for
natural disaster or serious illness with a doctor's certificate. If the
operator cancels a departure, you are told at least a week ahead and the full
amount is refunded or credited without any fee. All foreign passport holders
need a valid Indian visa before boarding a flight to India, and a refund
request arising from visa refusal falls under the cancellation rules above.

The full wording — and the equivalent terms for the other luxury trains we
book — is on our [booking terms](/booking-terms/) page.

## What we do, and what the operator does

India Visit is an **authorised booking agent** for the Palace on Wheels. The
train is owned and operated by RTDC, the Rajasthan Tourism Development
Corporation, Government of Rajasthan, and the operator's terms apply to your
journey. Our liability is that of booking agent. In practice that means we
arrange the booking, hold the cabin, handle the paperwork and look after you
around the journey; the operator runs the train.
