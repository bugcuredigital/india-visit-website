India rewards the well-prepared, and the preparation is less than people
fear. Twenty years of planning first visits have taught us that the same
handful of questions come up every time, so here they are, answered in the
order you will need them.

*Visa rules change. Everything below reflects the position at the time of
writing; we confirm the current requirements for your passport before you
travel.*

## The visa

Almost every foreign passport holder needs a visa to enter India, and for most
nationalities the route is the **e-visa**: applied for online, paid online,
and issued electronically before you fly. You will need a scan of your
passport's data page, a recent photograph, and your arrival airport, and the
processing time is usually a few days. Print the approval and carry it with
your passport; airlines check it at check-in.

The e-visa is valid for tourism and for the airports and seaports it lists.
It is not valid for every land border — if your journey crosses into Nepal or
Bhutan and back, tell us, because the re-entry needs thought.

## When to come

**October to March** is the season for most of the country: cool, dry and
clear across the north, Rajasthan and the south. Within it, **November to
February** is the sweet spot for the Golden Triangle and Rajasthan;
**December and January** can bring morning fog to the northern plains, which
delays road travel rather than ruining it.

The exceptions are the mountains. **Ladakh** is a June-to-September
destination — the passes are closed the rest of the year — and the hill
stations are at their best in the shoulder months. **Kerala** is pleasant from
October to March and green and wet through the monsoon. Wildlife reserves
close for the monsoon and are at their best, for sightings, in the hot months
of April and May.

## How long

Two weeks is the honest answer for a first visit that reaches more than one
region — a week in the north and a week in Kerala is the pairing we suggest
most. If you have less, one region done properly beats two done in a hurry:
six days is the Golden Triangle; a week is Kerala north to south. If you have
more, add a train, or the mountains.

## What a private journey includes

Every itinerary on this site is a private journey: your own car and driver
throughout, a local guide in each city, hotels chosen because we have stayed
in them, and every transfer, entrance and reservation arranged before you
arrive. It is not a group tour and there is no coach. The day-by-day plan is a
starting point — tell us what to change and we rebuild it around you.

What it does not include, unless the page says so, is international flights,
visa fees, travel insurance, lunches and tips. We are explicit about
inclusions on every journey page and in every quotation.

## Money

The currency is the Indian Rupee. Cards are accepted in hotels, larger
restaurants and city shops; carry cash for markets, small restaurants, temple
donations and tips. ATMs are common in cities and reliable in most towns.
Change money at the airport, a bank or your hotel, and keep the receipt.

## Health

See your doctor six to eight weeks before you travel for the standard advice
and any vaccinations. On the ground, the rules are simple and your driver and
guide will keep you to them: bottled or filtered water only, food that is hot
and freshly cooked, and a hat and water in the sun. Travel insurance is not
optional. Pharmacies are everywhere and good.

## The small things

- **Dress** modestly at religious sites — shoulders and knees covered — and
  carry a scarf; shoes come off at temples and some monuments.
- The **Taj Mahal is closed on Fridays**, and several museums close on
  Mondays. We plan around every one of these.
- **Bargaining** is expected in markets and not in shops with price tags.
- Your **phone** will work on roaming; a local SIM is cheap and worth the
  twenty minutes at the airport.
- **Photograph people only with permission**, and never at cremation ghats.
