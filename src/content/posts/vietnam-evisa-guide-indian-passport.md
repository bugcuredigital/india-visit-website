Vietnam is one of the best-value long journeys an Indian traveller can take,
and the paperwork is straightforward once you know its shape: an electronic
visa, applied for online before you fly, with a handful of details that must
match your booking exactly. Here is how it works, and what to decide first.

*Visa rules change. Everything below reflects the position at the time of
writing; we confirm the current requirements with you before you travel.*

## The e-visa

Indian passport holders enter Vietnam on an **e-visa**, applied for on the
Vietnamese government's immigration portal and issued electronically. There is
no visa on arrival for Indian nationals in the way Bali offers it; the e-visa
must be in hand before you board, and airlines check it at check-in.

What to have ready before you start the application:

- a scan of the **passport data page**, valid for at least six months beyond
  your arrival;
- a **passport-style photograph** to the portal's specification — this is the
  most common reason applications are sent back;
- your **entry and exit dates**, and the **port of entry** — the airport you
  will actually land at;
- a **hotel address** for the first night, which we supply.

The visa is tied to the details you enter. If you apply for Hanoi and land at
Ho Chi Minh City, or if the passport number has a typo, you will be refused at
the border. Check it twice; we check it a third time.

## How long it takes

The portal quotes a few working days. In practice we ask clients to apply at
least two weeks ahead, and earlier if the dates fall around **Tet** — the lunar
new year, usually late January or February — when much of the country closes
and processing slows. Print the approval letter and carry it with your
passport.

## Flights

Hanoi and Ho Chi Minh City are both reached from the Indian metros, direct or
with one connection. For the north-and-centre journey we run most often, the
useful routing is **into Hanoi and out of Da Nang**: the domestic flight
between them takes about ninety minutes and is far easier than connecting
through Ho Chi Minh City. Book the e-visa for the airport you land at.

## When to go — it depends where

Vietnam runs more than a thousand miles north to south, and the ends of it sit
in different climates.

**The north** — Hanoi and Ha Long Bay — is best from **October to April**.
Hanoi's winter can be damp and grey in February and March, so if your dates
are flexible aim for October, November or the spring.

**The centre** — Da Nang, Hoi An and Hue — sees heavy rain in **October and
November**. February to August is the drier window here, with the beach at its
best from May.

**The south** — Ho Chi Minh City and the Mekong — is warm all year, with the
dry season from December to April.

So a single "best time" does not exist. For the Hanoi–Ha Long–Hoi An journey,
we usually suggest **December to March**, when the north is cool and clear and
the centre has dried out; for a trip that reaches the south as well, the same
window holds. Tell us your dates and we will weight the itinerary north or
south to suit them.

## Money

The Vietnamese Dong runs to large numbers — a coffee costs tens of thousands —
so it pays to learn the notes early. Carry cash for markets and street food;
cards are accepted in hotels and larger restaurants. Change money at a bank or
a licensed counter, not on the street.

## A first-time week

The journey below is the one we recommend for a first visit: Hanoi's Old
Quarter and its food, a night on the water in Ha Long Bay, and then the flight
south for Da Nang's beach and the lantern-lit old town of Hoi An. One internal
flight, one night afloat, and the south left for another time.
