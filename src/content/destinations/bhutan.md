faq:
  - question: Is there a daily fee, and what does it cover?
    answer: >-
      Bhutan charges a Sustainable Development Fee per adult per night, paid to
      the government and separate from your travel costs. It funds healthcare,
      education and conservation. It is not optional and it is not a tax we can
      negotiate away, so we quote it plainly rather than burying it.
  - question: Do we need a visa and a guide?
    answer: >-
      Yes to both. Bhutan requires a visa arranged in advance through a
      licensed operator, and independent travel is not permitted — you travel
      with a licensed Bhutanese guide throughout. Indian, Bangladeshi and
      Maldivian nationals have a different arrangement.
  - question: How hard is the walk to the Tiger's Nest?
    answer: >-
      Four to five hours return, climbing around 700 metres, on a good path
      with a teahouse halfway. Reasonable fitness rather than mountaineering.
      Horses can be hired for the first half of the ascent but not the descent.
  - question: Can Bhutan be combined with India?
    answer: >-
      Commonly, via Delhi or Kolkata, and it pairs particularly well with a
      North East India route given the geography.

Bhutan is the rare destination where the restrictions are the reason to go. A
daily fee, a licensed guide and no independent travel add up to a country that
has not been reshaped around visitors, and it shows within an hour of landing.

The standard route runs Paro to Thimphu to Punakha and back, taking in the
dzongs, the Buddhist monastic calendar and — if the timing works — one of the
autumn tsechu festivals. The Tiger's Nest walk is the day everyone remembers,
and it is worth arranging the itinerary so that it falls late enough that you
are acclimatised and early enough that the weather is still on your side.
