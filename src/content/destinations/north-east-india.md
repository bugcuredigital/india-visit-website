faq:
  - question: Do I need a special permit?
    answer: >-
      Meghalaya and Assam do not require one. Arunachal Pradesh, Nagaland,
      Mizoram and Manipur do — an Inner Line Permit for Indian nationals and a
      Protected Area Permit for most foreign passports. We arrange them, but
      they need lead time, so tell us early if those states are on your list.
  - question: What are the living root bridges?
    answer: >-
      Ficus roots trained across streams by Khasi villagers over decades until
      they form a bridge that keeps growing stronger. The double-decker bridge
      at Nongriat is a 3,000-step descent and back; there are easier ones at
      Mawlynnong if that is not your day.
  - question: Is Cherrapunji really the wettest place on earth?
    answer: >-
      Cherrapunji and nearby Mawsynram trade the record between them, at around
      11,000mm a year. Almost all of it falls between May and September, which
      is exactly why we do not send people then.
  - question: How does this fit with a first trip to India?
    answer: >-
      It usually does not, and that is not a criticism. The North East rewards
      travellers who already know the country and want somewhere unlike it.

The North East is where India stops resembling itself. The hills are Khasi,
Garo and Naga; the architecture is timber and tin rather than sandstone; and the
rain, when it comes, arrives in quantities that have to be seen to be
understood.

Most journeys here centre on Meghalaya — Shillong, the Cherrapunji plateau, the
living root bridges at Nongriat and Mawlynnong — with Assam's tea estates and
Kaziranga's one-horned rhinos added where there is time. It is the quietest
travel we sell, and the travellers who take it tend to come back for the states
further east.
