faq:
  - question: How does a Kerala houseboat night actually work?
    answer: >-
      You board a converted kettuvallam in the afternoon at Alappuzha, cruise
      the canals until dusk, moor overnight and continue in the morning. The
      crew cooks on board, and the boat has private sleeping quarters, a dining
      area and a deck. Boats do not cruise after dark, which is why the
      mooring is part of the experience rather than a limitation.
  - question: Is Kerala a good fit for older travellers?
    answer: >-
      It is one of the better Indian regions for a gentler pace. Distances are
      short, roads are reasonable, and the itineraries build in genuine leisure
      days. The hill drives to Munnar and Thekkady are winding, so we plan them
      as morning departures with breaks.
  - question: Will we actually see wildlife at Periyar?
    answer: >-
      Elephants are commonly seen from the lake boat, along with sambar, wild
      boar and langur. Tigers are present in the reserve but sightings are rare
      and we never promise them. The boat safari is worth taking on its own
      terms.
  - question: How many days do the backwaters need?
    answer: >-
      One night aboard plus a day either side is enough for most travellers.
      Longer stays suit those who want to do very little, which is a perfectly
      good reason to extend.

Kerala rewards travellers who slow down. The state is narrow and green, stacked from the Arabian Sea up into the Western Ghats, and the distances are short enough that a twelve-day journey can take in a spice-trading port, a mile-high tea plantation, a tiger reserve and a night afloat without ever feeling rushed.

What stays with people is rarely a monument. It is the Chinese fishing nets working against the sunset at Fort Kochi, mist moving through the Kannan Devan Hills at first light, the sound of a houseboat crew cooking dinner while the canal goes quiet. Kerala is not a checklist destination, and the itineraries we build here are deliberately unhurried.
