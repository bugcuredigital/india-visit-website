Ladakh is the one region where the itinerary is dictated by physiology rather
than by taste. You fly into Leh at eleven and a half thousand feet, and for two
days you do very little — which turns out to be one of the better ways to meet
a place. Every Ladakh journey we sell has those days built in, and we will not
sell one without them.

After that it opens up: the great monasteries of the Indus valley at Shey,
Thiksey, Hemis and Spituk, the eleventh-century murals at Alchi, the moonscape
at Lamayuru, the villages along the river where the trip stops being
sightseeing. Beyond Leh lie the passes — the Khardung La into Nubra, the road
to Pangong, where the lake changes colour through the day and nothing about the
scale reads properly in photographs.

The journey below does something most Ladakh itineraries do not. It drives out
— west over the Zoji La into Kashmir, to Srinagar's lakes and Mughal gardens
and the meadow at Gulmarg — so that the bare rock of Ladakh and the green of
the valley are seen in one trip. That contrast is the thing people remember,
and it is only possible from June to September, when the pass is open. The
month-by-month guide below sets out the rest of the year.

A word on who this suits. With a doctor's clearance and a slower itinerary,
older travellers do well here; anyone with a heart or lung condition should
take advice first. The altitude is not negotiable, and we would rather have
that conversation early than halfway up a pass.
