faq:
  - question: How serious is the altitude?
    answer: >-
      Serious enough that we build two full rest days into the start of every
      Ladakh itinerary and will not sell one without them. Leh is at 11,500
      feet and you arrive by air, which gives your body no time to adjust. Most
      people are fine by day three; nobody is fine on day one.
  - question: When can you actually go?
    answer: >-
      June to September for the full region. Leh itself is reachable by air
      year-round, but the passes to Nubra and Pangong are snowbound outside the
      season and the lake road is not worth attempting on the margins.
  - question: Is it suitable for older travellers?
    answer: >-
      With a doctor's clearance and a slower itinerary, often yes — but the
      altitude is not negotiable and anyone with a heart or lung condition
      should take advice first. We would rather have that conversation early
      than halfway up a pass.
  - question: What is there beyond the monasteries?
    answer: >-
      Pangong and Tso Moriri, two high-altitude lakes that are worth the drive;
      the Nubra valley over the Khardung La; and the Indus valley villages,
      which are where the trip stops being sightseeing.

Ladakh is the one region where the itinerary is dictated by physiology rather
than by taste. You fly into Leh at eleven and a half thousand feet, and for two
days you do very little — which turns out to be one of the better ways to meet
a place.

After that it opens up: the great Gelugpa monasteries at Thiksey, Hemis and
Alchi, the Indus valley villages, the Khardung La into Nubra, and the drive out
to Pangong, where the lake changes colour through the day and nothing about the
scale reads properly in photographs.
